# **Real-Time Single-Lead ECG** Thesis Evidence Review

A low-cost single-lead ECG thesis built around Arduino, AD8232, and computer-side DSP is feasible for **real-time R-peak detection, approximate QRS delineation, basic signal-quality gating, and non-diagnostic rhythm warnings**, but it should keep **arrhythmia output rule-based**, treat **P and T detection as uncertainty-limited**, and present itself strictly as an **educational, non-medical system**. Pan–Tompkins and derivative-threshold families remain the most defensible undergraduate baseline because they are transparent, real-time, and well validated, while several lightweight newer variants improve speed or noise handling without changing the basic logic  (Pan & Tompkins, 1985; Arzeno et al., 2008; Gutierrez-Rivas et al., 2015; Neri et al., 2023).

## 1. Introduction

Single-lead wearable-style ECG analysis is dominated by a tradeoff between algorithmic simplicity and robustness to noise, motion, morphology variation, and limited sensing quality. The supplied literature is unusually consistent that **R-peak detection is the anchor task**, because heart rate, RR intervals, rhythm screening, and downstream delineation all depend on it  (Hao et al., 2024; Neri et al., 2023; Qin et al., 2017). The same literature is also consistent that high scores on MIT-BIH alone do not guarantee robustness on noisier wearable signals, exercise recordings, or low-quality Holter contexts  (Hao et al., 2024; Neri et al., 2023; Zahid et al., 2020).

For this thesis, the strongest evidence supports a **layered architecture**: robust filtering, transparent R-peak detection, constrained PQRST estimation, explicit signal-quality assessment, and conservative rule-based warnings rather than diagnosis  (Pandit et al., 2017; Bote et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2019; Karimipour & Homaeinezhad, 2014). Evidence also supports a narrow scope claim: QRS/R detection is mature enough for a student prototype, while reliable P and especially T/P delineation in noisy single-lead low-cost data remains more uncertain and should be framed as estimation rather than ground truth recovery  (Bote et al., 2018; Martínez et al., 2004; Karimipour & Homaeinezhad, 2014).

### 3.2 QRS Detector Comparison

The literature does not identify one universal best detector across all contexts. Pan–Tompkins remains a common benchmark because it combines band-pass filtering, slope/derivative information, squaring, and window integration in a real-time adaptive framework  (Park et al., 2017; Pan & Tompkins, 1985). Hamilton-style derivative methods remain attractive because they are computationally efficient and training-free, but they tend to suffer larger timing error and miss low-slope or wide abnormal beats  (Arzeno et al., 2008).

| Dimension | Pan–Tompkins Family | Lightweight Derivative/Threshold | Wavelet-Based | Citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core idea | Filter, derivative, square, integrate | Slope or derivative thresholds | Multiscale enhancement |  (Pan & Tompkins, 1985; Gutierrez-Rivas et al., 2015; Park et al., 2017)|
| Complexity | Low | Very low | Moderate |  (Qin et al., 2017; Gutierrez-Rivas et al., 2015; Merah et al., 2015)|
| Real-time fit | Strong | Strongest | Usually acceptable on PC |  (Pan & Tompkins, 1985; Gutierrez-Rivas et al., 2015; Park et al., 2017)|

The practical result is that **modified Pan–Tompkins or Hamilton-like detectors** are the best thesis default, while wavelet detectors are better reserved for optional comparison or offline benchmarking rather than as the primary real-time student implementation  (Neri et al., 2023; Gutierrez-Rivas et al., 2015; Park et al., 2017).

### 3.3 Delineation and SQI

PQRST delineation is much less secure than R-peak detection. Low-complexity delineators can estimate QRS, P, and T fiducials in real time, but even strong embedded delineation work reports that some fiducials, especially P onset, are harder to keep within accepted tolerances  (Bote et al., 2018). Wavelet delineation remains the classical high-performance reference and performs particularly well for T-wave end, but it is more complex than a minimal undergraduate pipeline needs  (Martínez et al., 2004). Template- and derivative-assisted real-time delineators can work, but some explicitly fall back to estimation when quality is poor, which is exactly the right safety framing for this project  (Karimipour & Homaeinezhad, 2014).

Signal-quality work strongly supports combining several simple SQIs rather than trusting one detector. Useful components include empty lead or spike criteria, crossing-point or morphology criteria, QRS-detection robustness, and segment acceptability classification  (Hayn et al., 2012; Liu et al., 2019). Several wearable-oriented papers further argue that **reliability of detected RR intervals** can matter as much as raw QRS accuracy in noisy real use  (Bae & Kwon, 2019).

### 3.4 Datasets and Real-Time Evaluation

MIT-BIH Arrhythmia remains the default benchmark for QRS detection, but it is not enough by itself because many algorithms exceed 99% there while degrading in noisy or wearable settings  (Rahman & Morshed, 2025; Zahid et al., 2020). QT Database is more useful for fiducial-point evaluation because it supports delineation benchmarking and morphology diversity  (Bote et al., 2018; Qin et al., 2017; Félix et al., 2023). MIT-BIH Noise Stress Test Database is the right benchmark for noise sensitivity, and several wavelet and robust wearable detectors use it explicitly to test degradation under realistic corruption  (Merah et al., 2015; Belkadi & Daamouche, 2021).

Real-time evaluation should not stop at Se and +P. Several papers explicitly include F1, timing error, RMS R-peak position error, processing time, or per-sample execution time, which fits this thesis well  (Neri et al., 2023; Rahman & Morshed, 2025; Tueche et al., 2021).

## 4. Discussion

The strongest overall conclusion is methodological, not just numerical: many detectors report Se and +P around or above 99% on standard datasets, so **context robustness, latency, interpretability, and implementation burden** become the real differentiators for an undergraduate system  (Bae & Kwon, 2019). That shifts the recommendation away from sophisticated deep models and toward transparent detectors with explicit SQI gating.

The evidence for using ML in this thesis is mixed. Lightweight embedded ML for arrhythmia classification is possible, and recent work shows that wearables can host small CNN/LSTM models with acceptable latency  (Rahman & Morshed, 2025). But the same corpus repeatedly notes that ML increases data-label requirements, evaluation burden, and risk of overclaiming when training is limited or datasets are imbalanced or unrealistically clean  (Rahman & Morshed, 2025; Zahid et al., 2020).

The literature also supports a safety-first reporting strategy. Real-time systems can issue rhythm-related warnings, but several studies rely on template correlation, RR plausibility, or quality thresholds before trusting those outputs, which argues for a visible **“poor signal / unreliable analysis”** state in the thesis UI  (Bamarouf et al., 2016; Karimipour & Homaeinezhad, 2014; Hayn et al., 2012).

## 5. Conclusion

The literature supports a bounded but strong thesis contribution: an Arduino–AD8232 single-lead system can support real-time acquisition, filtering, visualization, R-peak detection, QRS-centered feature extraction, and conservative educational warnings, provided the system explicitly tracks signal quality and does not claim diagnosis  (Chen & Chuang, 2017; Chen et al., 2006; Liu et al., 2019). The best implementation choice is a **modified Pan–Tompkins or Hamilton-style detector on the PC side**, with **windowed Q/S search**, **cautious P/T estimation**, **multi-feature SQI**, and **rule-based warning logic**.

### Research Gaps

The main gaps are not whether R-peaks can be detected, but how well low-cost single-lead systems generalize to motion, poor electrode contact, and full fiducial delineation.

### Open Research Questions

Future work is most valuable where low-cost sensing, uncertainty handling, and wearable noise intersect.

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| **How accurately can simple SQI-gated rule-based warnings suppress false alarms in low-cost single-lead ECG during motion?** | This directly determines whether educational warnings remain credible outside clean benchmark recordings. |
| **Which low-complexity P/T estimation strategy is most stable after robust R detection in noisy single-lead AD8232 data?** | P and T remain the least secure parts of the proposed thesis pipeline. |
| **How much improvement do modified Pan–Tompkins variants provide over classical Pan–Tompkins on real Arduino-acquired noise?** | This is a focused, thesis-sized comparison with clear implementation and evaluation value. |

A rigorous thesis should therefore present **real-time R-peak detection and conservative warning logic as the core result**, and treat **full PQRST delineation and arrhythmia interpretation as secondary, uncertainty-bounded features**.
